# COVID-19 and excess mortality among children and young people in the US, 2020–2023

Reproducible code and data for the manuscript *COVID-19 deaths and excess mortality among children and young people (ages 0–24) in the United States, 2020–2023: variation by age, state, region and race/ethnicity* (under review).

Everything in the paper — five figures, two appendix tables, the numbers quoted in the Results and the sensitivity analyses — is produced from the CDC WONDER exports in `data_input/` by eleven R scripts run in sequence.

## Quick start

```r
# in R, from the project root (open us_youth_pandemic_mortality.Rproj)
renv::restore()        # installs the package versions in renv.lock (first time only)
source("run_all.R")    # ~1 minute
```

or from a shell: `Rscript run_all.R`. On the first run script 06 downloads the US state boundaries from the Census Bureau (via `tigris`) and caches them in `data_inter/us_states_cb_2022.rds`; every later run is offline.

Developed with R 4.6.1 on Windows; package versions are pinned in `renv.lock`.

## Data

All inputs are public CDC WONDER exports (tab-separated `.txt`, each with WONDER's query footer kept intact), queried between 4 June and 5 August 2024. See [`data_input/README.md`](data_input/README.md) for the query parameters of every file and [`data_input/wonder_queries.csv`](data_input/wonder_queries.csv) for a machine-readable index.

- **Deaths** — *Provisional Mortality Statistics, 2018 through Last Week* (2020–2023; 2023 was provisional at query time) and *Multiple Cause of Death, 1999–2020* (2010–2020, final). COVID-19 deaths are those with underlying cause ICD-10 U07.1.
- **Population** — *Single-Race Population Estimates 2020–2022* (by race/ethnicity and age) and *State Population Projections 2004–2030* (by state, sex and age).

WONDER suppresses counts below 10. Age-specific counts for states and race/ethnic groups are therefore recovered by querying open-ended age groups (all known ages, 1+, 5+, 10+, …), which are never suppressed, and differencing them (scripts 01–03).

## Methods in brief

- **Excess deaths** = observed − baseline, by calendar year 2020–2023. The baseline is a quasi-Poisson GLM, log-linear in year with a log-population offset, fitted to 2010–2019 and projected forward (`est_baseline_pi()` in `code/00_functions.R`). It is fitted separately for each US age group, each state × age group, each state for ages 0–24 combined, each BEA region for ages 0–24 combined, and each state for ages 25+ and all ages.
- **Intervals.** The scripts compute both a 95 % confidence interval of the baseline (`lc`, `uc`; ± 1.96 SE of the fitted value) and a 95 % prediction interval (`lp`, `up`; 200 draws of the coefficients from their sampling distribution, each combined with negative-binomial noise using the estimated dispersion; seed 2024). **The figures and tables show the confidence interval** (observed − `uc`, observed − `lc`). The prediction interval, which also accounts for the Poisson-type variability of the observed counts and is therefore wider, is kept in `data_inter/` and compared with the CI in Figure S1 and `output/sensitivity_summary.txt`.
- **Regions** follow the Bureau of Economic Analysis grouping (Table A1). Regional COVID-19 deaths, excess deaths and populations are sums over states; regional interval bounds are sums of the state bounds. A baseline fitted directly on regional totals is also estimated (script 04) and compared in script 10.
- **Relative risk** (Figure 4) = race/ethnicity-specific COVID-19 death rate ÷ all-races rate, same age group and year.
- **CYP in the context of all-age excess** (script 11). P-scores (observed / expected − 1) by state for ages 0–24, 25+ and all ages, directly age-standardised to the 2020 US population (seven groups: 0–1 … 20–24, 25+), with the CI of the baseline carried through the standardisation; the CYP share of each state's excess deaths and of its COVID-19 deaths, crude and age-standardised (the share CYP would have if the state had the US age structure: the state's age-specific rates applied to the US 2020 population; in the maps, states whose all-age excess CI includes zero are greyed out because the share is then a ratio of two small numbers); and the difference between each state's CYP p-score and the national one. Ages 25+ are one group because the state exports do not split adults further, so "old-age" excess cannot be separated from working-age excess. The 25+ and all-age denominators are WONDER's postcensal estimates, whereas the CYP series uses the state projections (note 4).
- **Sensitivity analyses** (script 10). Figure S1: US excess by age with the log-linear baseline fitted to 2010–2019 (main), 2014–2019 or 2015–2019, each with CI and PI. Figure S2: observed death rates 2010–2023 with the three fitted baselines, showing where the windows diverge. Figure S3: rolling-origin validation — the baseline is fitted to years up to 2015, 2016 and 2017 under each strategy (all years since 2010 / last 6 years / last 5 years) and projected to 2019, giving out-of-sample errors at horizons of 1–4 years (`output/sensitivity_cv_rolling_origin.csv`). Also: state 0–24 excess with the baseline fitted on the aggregate (main) vs. summed over the age-specific baselines; number of states with significant excess under CI vs. PI; regional intervals from summed state bounds vs. a regional fit. Results in `output/sensitivity_summary.txt`.

## Methodological notes and known data issues

Known limitations of the input data and of the estimation, in the order they affect the pipeline.

1. **Provisional 2023 data.** All 2023 death counts come from the provisional file as of July–August 2024; final 2023 data are now available on WONDER and would change counts slightly.
2. **Query drift.** The open-ended age queries for states were run on different days against the still-changing provisional file. For Arkansas, Nevada and Washington in 2023 the "1+" count exceeds the "known age" count by 1–2, which yields −1/−2 infant deaths (script 01 warns about this). Effect on 0–24 totals: ≤ 2 deaths.
3. **2023 populations by race/ethnicity** were not available at query time; 2022 values are carried forward (script 02).
4. **State denominators** for ages 0–24 are from WONDER's *State Population Projections 2004–2030* rather than postcensal estimates. Script 03 compares the projected 0–24 population with the postcensal one implied by the all-age and 25+ exports: they agree within 5 % for most state-years, but the projections are too high by up to 25 % (Arizona, Nevada, 2023) and too low by up to 42 % (North Dakota) in later years. A level error cancels in observed/expected ratios; a trend error does not.
5. **The fitting window matters at ages 20–24** (Figures S1–S2). Mortality at 20–24 rose until 2017 and fell in 2018–2019, so the slope extrapolated to 2020–2023 depends on the window (+1.6 %/yr for 2010–2019, −0.5 %/yr for 2015–2019) and the 2023 excess ranges from −1,533 (main) to +895. The out-of-sample check (2018–2019 predicted from 2010–2017 vs. shorter windows) favours the longest window, but every window over-predicts 2018–2019 deaths at 15–24 by 7–20 %. Ages 1–19 are robust to the choice.
6. **Confidence vs. prediction intervals.** The CI of the baseline shown in the figures does not include the Poisson variability of the observed counts; the prediction interval does and is about 1.5× wider at the state level, which changes the significance of some cells (`output/sensitivity_summary.txt`, section 3).
7. **Zero cells.** Relative risks of 0 (no deaths) are omitted from Figure 4; rates of change with a zero denominator (infinite) or outside the axis range are dropped from Figure 5 (non-Hispanic Asian panel).

## Repository layout

```
code/              numbered scripts, run in order by run_all.R
data_input/        CDC WONDER exports (inputs) + README and query index; all_cause_race/ = API pulls for script 12
data_inter/        intermediate .rds produced by scripts 01–04 (+ cached state boundaries)
figures/           fig1 … fig5 and figS1 … figS3 (.png) with their data (.csv), the versions in the paper; alternatives/ holds the alternative versions drawn by the alt_fig* scripts (fig1_altA, fig2_altA/B, with their .csv); exploratory/ holds the race exploration of script 12 (figR1–R3)
tables/            Tables A1 and A2 (.docx and .csv), Table S1 (.csv)
output/            numbers quoted in the text; sensitivity summary
renv.lock          pinned package versions
```

The repository's earlier content (a 2023 analysis of ICD-coded mortality microdata by race, 1999–2021) is preserved under the git tag `legacy-2023-microdata`.
